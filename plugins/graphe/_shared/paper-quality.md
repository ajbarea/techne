# Paper quality

Canonical rules for writing and reviewing a research paper. Read with
`_shared/plain-prose.md` (sentence-level rules) by `/graphe:paper` before
scaffolding and by `/graphe:paper-review` on every pass. Update this file, not
the skills.

## The bar

The bar is clarity, quality and conciseness. Reviewers, venue conventions and
advisors are lenses for finding real weaknesses, never an audience to cater to.
A change goes in only if it makes the paper clearer, more rigorous or more
concise. Prefer removing or sharpening over adding.

## Every sentence earns its place

A sentence stays if it helps a reader judge one of the venue's review criteria
or the call requires it. Otherwise cut it or merge it into a neighbour.

- Log every cut verbatim in `CUTS.md` with a one-line reason, so the author can
  restore any line. `CUTS.md` is a working file: keep it out of the submission.
- Someone other than the editor audits the cuts against the call's required
  elements. An editor checking their own cuts confirms their intent.

## Prose

Plain, clear, concise, and fun to read. Nothing flowery: no ornate phrasing,
stacked adjectives, or filler such as "crucially", "notably", "novel",
"comprehensive". Claim novelty by stating what is new, in numbers or contrasts.

## Learn the genre from accepted papers

The target venue matters more than the lab's own style. Before drafting, read
the venue's accepted papers for structure, never prose:

- titles, abstract moves and length, how RQs, hypotheses and contributions are
  stated, the section skeleton, tense;
- record the counts in `conventions.md` beside the paper (venue, papers read,
  title patterns, abstract length range, RQ phrasing, section order, tense);
- quote titles only. Never copy a sentence.

## Voice

The author writes or dictates the claim-bearing sentences: motivation, main
finding, why it matters. The assistant does structure, editing, evidence
tracing, citation checks and critique. LLM revision homogenizes style (Lin and
Zhu, arXiv 2504.13629), so a rewrite of an author's claim sentence is a
suggestion shown beside the original, never a silent replacement.

## Integrity guards

- Never state an ethics or IRB determination the author does not hold in
  writing. Write a placeholder and flag it.
- Never report readings or numbers from data the author lacks permission to
  use. Where the paper promises that something is not in a published figure,
  check every figure, table, caption and appendix keeps that promise.

## Registered reports

Stage 1 plan: future tense. Finished work (pilots, tooling, prior studies):
past tense and labelled as finished. No results in the abstract.
